# Bidaya Consulting — The three audiences

**Phase 3 revisit, part 1 — v1.0, 23 September 2026.** Produced with `target-audience`, run three times, against the founder's answers of 23 September (ledger BG, BH; his six answers on the audiences the same evening), the round-one audience section of the strategy lock v2.2, and Draft 2 §2 as amended by ledger BR. One profile per audience, each its own person, as the founder asked: "each one of these targets a different person entirely, and it's important we log exactly who we're targeting and why."

**What is his and what is mine.** Everything in his words is marked as his. Every claim about where these people are, what they read and how they decide that he did not state is a **[hypothesis]** — round one had no customer research and round two still has none; ten conversations per audience would replace the hypotheses with facts. Nothing below is copy. The audience pages' words are written in Phase 7 against these profiles.

**The predictable version, named and rejected.** Three stock personas — the young startup founder, the aspiring entrepreneur, the institutional partner — each with a first name, a coffee habit and "values authenticity." Every consultancy's deck has them and none of them decide anything. These profiles are built the other way round: from the specific places the founder named (Al Quoz, Alserkal Avenue, CAAD at AUS, the two festivals), the specific jobs each person needs done, and the sentence each one says out loud — so that a line of copy, a form option or a program pitch can be tested against a person rather than a category.

## 1. Founders & SMEs

### 01 — Overview

The owner of a UAE business that is trading and has money behind it — seed-funded or funded by its own revenue — who is carrying every part of the company personally, at the expense of the thing they are good at. They are past the idea and past the licence. What they lack is not ability; it is an executive layer, and they cannot justify three senior hires to get one. What frustrates them about the market: firms that sell a "CFO" label over bookkeeping, and firms that place a senior person who advises but delivers nothing beneath the seat. Round one's audience stands; the founder's answer 6 was "No" to any change.

### 02 — Profile

- **Demographics (descriptive, never a rule):** owners and founders, roughly 25–45; Sharjah, Dubai, Abu Dhabi; a company with staff and revenue; budget for a monthly engagement of AED 12,000–30,000 or a defined piece of work from AED 7,000 (internal floor — never on a linked page, BM).
- **Psychographics:** they want speed of *execution* — things off their plate, done properly — not speed of *commitment*; they respect a study before a recommendation; they resent being called a startup if they aren't one and resent hoodie language if they are. They decide on mechanisms, not adjectives.
- **Behaviour:** reads the whole terms page; compares against the cost of a hire; books a call when a number and a term are on it. Trigger: a failure state with a number attached — a fine, an unexplained losing quarter, an investor asking for reporting that doesn't exist. **[hypothesis]** Finds Bidaya through a search for a fractional CFO or COO in the UAE, a referral from an accountant or a peer, or an institution's program they once sat in.

### 03 — Personas (revised, not replaced)

**Rania, 32 — founder, seed-funded logistics-software company, Dubai.** Nine staff, eighteen months in. Product and customers by day; invoices, visas and an unwritten hiring plan by night. *"I know exactly what the product needs. I have no idea if the company is profitable."* Needs the executive layer that reports to her, not a vendor that bills her. **What changed since round one:** she no longer meets *Where you stand* on the site, and no written "you may not need us" — she converts on the free call, the trial month with no notice, the 30-day notice, and a proposal in writing. Hesitates at any menu, package, or a "we" that is one person. → Monthly.

**Khalid, 41 — owner, contracting and fit-out business, Sharjah.** Twelve years, forty staff, run from his phone. Margins moved and nobody can say why; his accountant records, no one explains. *"The business is good. The company is a mess."* Needs a study of how the company actually runs before anyone recommends anything. **What changed:** the paid diagnostic is no longer named on the site; he converts on the free call and a fixed-scope proposal — a study, a structure, a hand-over. Hesitates at startup coding. → One defined piece of work first; monthly only if that is the honest conclusion. **He is why the audience is "Founders & SMEs" and why the page reads to owners, not only founders.**

### 04 — Their language

*Problem:* "It's getting too big to handle on my own." "Too much on my plate." "I don't know if we're making money." "Not get fined for something I didn't know about." *Outcome:* "Done properly." "A few steps ahead of me." "Stay on the business." *Why they've chosen others:* "The accountant does it" (records, doesn't explain); "we hired a manager" (too early, too junior). **Implication:** the Founders page is the one place on the site where the executive-layer argument runs in full; it is written to Khalid as much as Rania.

### 05 — Not for

The idea-stage founder with no company yet (→ Creatives & pre-preneurs — no longer "not for" the firm, Draft 3 §2); the owner who wants to skip the study; sub-floor monthly budgets for ongoing work; the formation-mill buyer; anyone wanting a marketing, design or PR agency; companies with a full executive team.

### 06 — Implications

**Message:** lead with the executive layer inside the business; the four functions as scopes of one role; the terms as proof. **Channel:** [hypothesis] search on the function pages ("Fractional COO in the UAE"), referrals, the institutions' programs as a slow feeder. **Voice:** executive, not vendor; stage, not size — for Claude's drafting (BL). **Experience:** the free call must produce a proposal quickly; the questionnaire's business questions are the intake.

## 2. Creatives & pre-preneurs

### 01 — Overview

Two people share this page and one sentence joins them: *you signed up to be an artist, not a businessperson.* The first is the **practising creative** — the painter, designer, maker, ceramicist, photographer, the person making unique or handmade pieces, the one who teaches art — trying to make a good living from work they already do well, and finding that the company side (licensing, pricing, invoicing, the numbers, the first hire) is the part no one taught them. The second is the **pre-preneur** — the founder's word — a person who wants to start a business and lacks the experience: often young, not always; a full-time employee planning to leave; a parent at home who wants to build their own thing; a student in business, design, arts, management, or any field at all. Neither has an executive layer and neither needs one yet. What they need is the support system a first business needs, and someone who will do the parts they can't — for a fee, one piece at a time.

The founder's own framing, verbatim: "It's difficult to commercialize on art as an artist, you signed up to be an artist, not a businessperson, but you still want to make a good living from your creative works, that's where I can step in."

### 02 — Profile

- **Demographics:** **18 and over** — the founder's rule: "I will most definitely cater to people who can legally own a business… Yes younger may find interest, but not the target audience." No upper age: "the audience is what matters, not the age." The majority of pre-preneurs are young; "a lot of older people too could be in this, and in the creatives section." UAE-wide; the named places are Al Quoz and Alserkal Avenue in Dubai and the College of Architecture, Art and Design at AUS in Sharjah, and universities in every field. **They pay:** "Ideally they pay… I'm looking for them to be paying — or whoever is setting up their business." One-time engagements from AED 3,500 (`/fees` only), the free 30-minute call, the community's guides and tools.
- **Psychographics (the founder's, and hypotheses):** they identify by their craft or their idea, not as "entrepreneurs"; the word *business* is something that happens to them, not something they chose. They want to keep making and to be paid properly for it. They distrust anything that looks like a course-selling funnel. **[hypothesis]** They trust peers and places (a studio neighbour at Alserkal, a lecturer at CAAD) far more than firms; a Bidaya workshop they attended is the reference, not a website.
- **Behaviour:** **[hypothesis]** They arrive from a workshop, a festival, a university event, a referral inside the creative community, or a search for a specific task ("how to get a freelance permit", "how much does a trade licence cost"). They decide slowly and on trust; they pay for one clear thing, not a monthly relationship (the founder: "one-time approach, almost never monthly"). The buying process: attend or read → the free call → a proposal for one piece of work → done.
- **Where they are (the founder's list):** "Work in or always hang around Al Quoz / Al Serkal, in CAAD AUS, in different universities studying business, design, arts, management, all different forms of studies… They're also people employed and working full time but looking to leave that, or mothers and parents who are stay at homes that want to explore learning how to start their own thing, they're everywhere."

### 03 — Personas

**Maha, 29 — ceramicist and part-time art teacher, studio in Al Quoz, Dubai.** Sells pieces through her Instagram and two shops, teaches weekend classes, gets asked for commissions she prices by instinct. No licence of her own yet; every payment is a favour or a personal transfer. *"I can make anything. I can't tell you what my month looks like or whether I should be charging double."* **Goals:** a legal way to sell and invoice; a price for her work she can defend; classes that pay for the studio. **Frustrations:** every formation company sells her a package she doesn't understand; the free advice online is for software startups; the one accountant she asked spoke in acronyms. **Needs from Bidaya:** one person who explains the company side in her terms and does the parts she can't — the permit, the invoice template, the first budget — for a price she knows before she starts. **Triggers:** a commission too big to do off the books; a gallery asking for a trade licence; a Bidaya workshop she attended. **Hesitates at:** anything monthly; anything that sounds like consulting for companies; a form that asks about her "budget" before it asks about her work. **Channels:** [hypothesis] Alserkal's programs and open studios, Instagram, the art-school network, the makers' markets. **How Bidaya solves it:** the company side of the craft, done once, priced from AED 3,500, by people who have run businesses — so she keeps making.

**Omar, 36 — marketing manager at a bank, Dubai; wants to leave to start a business.** Fourteen years employed, a plan for a specialist food business, savings set aside, no idea of the order of things. *"I know how to run a team. I don't know how to start a company — the licence, the bank account, what comes first."* **Goals:** a sequence — what to do first, what it costs, when to resign; a feasibility read before he commits; the setup done right the first time. **Frustrations:** the formation mills quote him five prices for the same licence; friends' advice contradicts itself; every setup page that promises three steps is a lead form. **Needs from Bidaya:** the sequence and the study — someone who has done the beginning many times and will tell him the cost of each step, and who will do the setup when he says go. **Triggers:** a date to resign; a lease he is about to sign; a partner asking for a plan. **Hesitates at:** being treated as a startup founder (he isn't yet) or as a creative (he isn't); any promise of an outcome. **Channels:** [hypothesis] LinkedIn, search for setup costs, a Bidaya talk at a company or a university event. **How Bidaya solves it:** a feasibility study and a setup plan as one piece of work; then, if the business grows, the executive layer — he is the pre-preneur who becomes a founder.

**Layla, 42 — mother of three, at home, Sharjah; wants to start a home-based business.** Trained as a graphic designer, out of work for nine years by choice, now building a stationery and gifting brand from her kitchen table. *"I want to do this properly, but I don't know what properly means here — do I need a licence for this? What can I even do from home?"* **Goals:** to know what is legal and what it costs; to sell online with a proper invoice; a small, real business that fits around the family. **Frustrations:** information written for men in offices; every service assumes she has capital and staff; nobody answers a small question without selling a package. **Needs from Bidaya:** a guide she can read tonight; a workshop she can attend in a morning; one call where someone tells her the three things to do first — and, when she is ready, someone to do the setup. **Triggers:** an order she can't invoice; a marketplace asking for a licence number; a friend who did it with Bidaya. **Hesitates at:** prices before conversation; anything that requires her to call herself an entrepreneur; a login before she has seen one useful thing. **Channels:** [hypothesis] parenting and community groups, Instagram, school networks, a community's guides shared by a friend. **How Bidaya solves it:** the guides and the community first, the call second, one piece of work when she's ready — the support a first business needs, from day one.

**Also on this page — the student.** The CAAD design student, the AUS business student, the graduate of any field with an idea: reached through the universities and the institutions' programs (audience 3), served through the workshops, the guides and the community, rarely a paying client until they leave. The page speaks to them without being written to them; the institutions' programs are where Bidaya meets them.

### 04 — Their language

*Problem:* "I have an amazing business idea and no clue where to begin." (the founder's most-heard sentence) · "I signed up to be an artist, not a businessperson." (his framing) · "I don't know what I'm allowed to do from home." · "Do I need a licence for this?" · "What comes first?" *Outcome:* "Do it properly." "Make a living from what I make." "Know what it costs before I start." "Someone to just do it." *Why they've chosen others:* "The formation company was cheap" · "I asked a friend" · "I watched videos." **Implication:** the page opens on the craft or the idea, never on "entrepreneurship"; the questions in the form ask about the work before the money; the community's first useful thing is free and visible before any registration.

### 05 — Not for

Anyone under 18 (they may read; the offer is for people who can own a business); the practising creative who wants a manager or an agent; the person looking for funding or a grant through Bidaya ("I'm not linking them up with grants yet"); the trading business that already has staff and revenue — that is Founders & SMEs, and the page hands them across without a downgrade in either direction.

### 06 — Implications

**Message:** the support a first business needs; the company side of the craft; one piece at a time; the free call. Never the executive layer (they don't need one yet, and the phrase belongs to Founders). **Channel:** the institutions' programs and the two festivals are the front door to this audience at scale; Alserkal and CAAD by name in outreach, never as claims on the site. **Voice:** the same register, warmer — Rule 3 (stretched, never incapable) matters most here: nobody on this page "doesn't know how"; they haven't been taught the company yet. **Experience:** the questionnaire's general questions; the community with a visible free layer before any login (the founder's likely choice is registration; the first useful thing must sit in front of it); the one-time floor on `/fees` only.

## 3. Institutions

### 01 — Overview

The person whose job is programs, events or entrepreneurship inside an institution: a head of programs at a cultural foundation; a university's president's office, events coordinator or entrepreneurship-centre manager; a government entity's initiatives team; an incubator or accelerator looking for trainings and workshops; a festival's program director. They already run the structures — residencies, cohorts, competitions, festivals, campaigns — and they need a partner who can build and run the business side of them, bring people and speakers, manage the project, and bring tech and AI into the institution itself. They buy on relationships and reputation, in meetings; the site is where they verify. There is no price for them on the site, ever (BM).

The founder's intent, verbatim: "I want them to see the vision I have and what I want to work on, making it easier for people to set up and run businesses in the UAE, and supporting them to run with sustainable growth, make profitable, and encourage youth and creatives to start businesses."

### 02 — Profile

- **Who signs (the founder's list):** "Head of programs, presidents of universities / university events coordinators / program managers / events or project managers / government entities / event coordinators / incubators looking for trainings or workshops and more."
- **What they buy, in their words (the founder's answer 5):** "A partner that offers all of these things, supplies people, manpower, tech solutions etc; a potential speaker / provider of speakers; a project manager / coordinator; a tech consultant who optimizes, automates and educates on AI. All of these are important things to sell to institutions."
- **The kind of thing (his examples):** the Al Quoz Creative Entrepreneurship Festival, the Sharjah Entrepreneurship Festival, "or many others like that" — as a speaker, a workshop or talk, and "hopefully at some point partner with and bring in a collective of businesses from different industries to support running it"; programs for youth and creatives in partnership with Bidaya and major companies at universities (competitions, workshops, talks); tech automation and integration; trainings for institutions and their employees on tech and AI; software implementation and operations support; project and partnership management with smaller businesses and artists.
- **Psychographics [hypothesis]:** they measure in outcomes they can report — participants, partners, a program delivered on time, a competition that produced businesses — and in the reputation of who they worked with; they are risk-averse about vendors and open to partners; they resent being pitched services and welcome being offered a program.
- **Behaviour [hypothesis]:** planning cycles are academic years and event calendars; decisions involve a committee and a procurement step; the first contact is a meeting, an introduction or a speaker slot, not a form. The site's job is to make the meeting go well.

### 03 — Personas

**Noor, 36 — head of programs, cultural foundation, Dubai (round one, revised).** A residency of practitioners excellent at their craft and lost on invoicing, licensing, pricing; a board that wants viable businesses out of it. *"We can teach them craft. We can't teach them to run a company."* **What changed:** she now also needs the program itself run — the business-foundations track designed and delivered inside her residency, the speakers found, the partners brought in — and she is a route to Maha and her neighbours at scale. **Needs from Bidaya:** a partner in her institution's register who co-designs and delivers; no price on the page; a track record she can show her board (BK: none yet — she converts on the meeting and the first delivered workshop). **Triggers:** a cohort about to start; a board asking for outcomes; a festival with an empty business-track slot. **Hesitates at:** anything that reads as services to institutions; a price; startup coding.

**Amal, 33 — manager, entrepreneurship centre, a university in Sharjah.** Runs the competition, the speaker series and the incubation room; reports to a dean; has a budget for events and trainings and a mandate to produce founders from students in every college. *"I have the students and the room. I need the people who have actually done it — and someone to run the whole thing so I'm not doing it on top of my job."* **Goals:** a competition or program with real companies in it; workshops the students rate; a partner who also brings sponsors and speakers. **Frustrations:** consultants who send a deck and a day-rate; speakers who inspire and leave; vendors who don't understand a university's calendar or procurement. **Needs from Bidaya:** a program built with her, delivered inside her calendar, with Bidaya as the operator and the network — companies, speakers, the collective — behind it; and, separately, someone who can train her own staff on AI and fix the tools they use. **Triggers:** the next academic year's plan; a dean's push for entrepreneurship numbers; a company offering to sponsor a competition with no one to run it. **Hesitates at:** a fee before a scope; a partner who wants her students as leads. **Channels:** [hypothesis] the university network, LinkedIn, the founder's own board role, the festivals. **How Bidaya solves it:** the program from ideation to launch, with the people and the partners, so the centre delivers more than it staffs.

**Saeed, 40 — initiatives manager, a government entrepreneurship or SME entity, an emirate-level body.** Owns a campaign or a festival with public targets: more licences, more youth businesses, more creative-sector companies. *"We have the stage and the mandate. We need people who can put a real program under it and run it properly, and who can show us what the small businesses actually need."* **Goals:** a campaign or festival that delivers a program, not only a stage; trainings for the entity's staff on AI and automation; a partner who brings the small businesses and the collective with it. **Frustrations:** big consultancies that price for government and deliver slides; suppliers who can't talk to a maker and a minister in the same week; slow procurement that needs a clear scope. **Needs from Bidaya:** a scoped program with a named operator; the collective of businesses as the delivery network; tech and AI capability the entity can use itself. **Triggers:** an annual festival's planning cycle; a national or emirate youth-entrepreneurship push; a tender for trainings. **Hesitates at:** a firm with no track record on the site (BK — the risk is his to accept); a price list; anything that looks like a startup pitching. **Channels:** [hypothesis] introductions, the festivals, the entity's own calls for partners. **How Bidaya solves it:** a partner that supplies the program, the people and the tech under the entity's banner, and reports outcomes in the entity's terms.

**Also on this page — the incubator.** An accelerator or incubator manager "looking for trainings or workshops": the shortest engagement, a workshop or a series, often the first meeting with an institution. Served by the same partnerships questions; reached through the same network.

### 04 — Their language

*Problem (hypotheses in their register):* "We can teach the craft, not the company." "We have the mandate and the room; we don't have the people." "Our founders leave the program without a licence." "Nobody on our team understands the tools." *Outcome:* "A program we can report on." "Delivered inside our calendar." "The right partners in the room." "Our staff can actually use it." *Why they've chosen others:* "They had a track record" — the sentence Bidaya cannot yet answer with proof (BK). **Implication:** the Institutions page speaks the partner's register — partners, practitioners, programs, co-delivered — and never the vendor's; Rule 14 stands; the offer list (BH) is presented as what a partnership contains, not as a price list.

### 05 — Not for

Institutions that want fractional executive services (that is a company, not an institution — hand them to Founders & SMEs); institutions expecting a price list on the site; sponsors looking for Bidaya to fund a program (funding is co-funded — Bidaya, sponsors, partners — strategy lock §12); a school seeking to put under-18s into a paid Bidaya program (the offer is for the institution; the participants are the institution's).

### 06 — Implications

**Message:** the vision first — the founder's words — then what a partnership contains: programs, events and campaigns built and run; speakers and people; project and partnership management; tech and AI for the institution. **Channel:** meetings, introductions, speaker slots and the festivals; the site verifies, the meeting converts; the founder's own network and board roles are the first channel [hypothesis, founder to confirm]. **Voice:** institutional register; partner, never vendor; no price, no "services". **Experience:** the partnerships questions in the questionnaire; the call; a proposal. The Programs page's rule (no price, no services) transfers to the Institutions page in full.

## 4. What crosses the three

- **One sentence reaches all three:** *"I have an amazing business idea and no clue where to begin"* is Omar's and Layla's; *"This is harder than I thought and is too big to handle on my own"* is Rania's and Khalid's; Noor, Amal and Saeed are the people who hear both sentences from the people they serve. The story (Phase 4) is built on this.
- **Two routes feed each other:** institutions are how Bidaya meets creatives and pre-preneurs at scale; creatives and pre-preneurs who grow become founders. Neither route is a funnel dressed as support (strategy lock §12 stands).
- **The Talent Network sits behind all three** as a mechanism — the people Bidaya brings in — and is never an audience (BH). *Work with us* is its page.
- **Proof at launch is none for all three** (BK). The free call, the terms, the seven and the founder's writing carry it; the institutions are the audience where this costs most and where the first delivered workshop matters more than any page.
