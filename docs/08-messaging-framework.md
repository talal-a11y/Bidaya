## 01 — Core message

*Bidaya helps founders and owners of UAE businesses run properly before they can hire an executive team, by acting as their executive layer across setup, operations, finance and tech, monthly or for one defined piece of work, on terms it publishes.*

Everything else is a version of this sentence.

---

## 02 — Value proposition

Bidaya is the executive layer for businesses that don't have one yet. For founders and owners of funded startups and established SMEs in the UAE, it runs the operating side — setup, operations, finance and tech — the way an executive would, so the owner's time goes back to the work the business exists to do. Every other firm in the market is either the seat without the hands or the hands without the seat; Bidaya is both, and it publishes the terms that prove it.

**Spoken — ten seconds:** *We're the executive layer for businesses that don't have one yet. We run the operating side, setup through tech, so the owner doesn't have to.*

**Spoken — thirty seconds:** *Most businesses run for years before they can justify three senior hires. Until then the owner carries finance, operations, structure and systems alone. Bidaya runs that side inside the business, monthly or for one piece of work. The first month has no notice attached, every engagement can end in thirty days, and if you don't need us on retainer we say so in writing. We'd rather you had the right structure than a contract.*

## 03 — Tagline and hero: decided

**Tagline:** ***Executive management, from the beginning.***
**Hero headline:** ***Setup, operations, finance and tech — run inside your business by one firm.***

The tagline says what Bidaya *is* and *when*; the hero says what it *does*, in the canonical order, immediately (§9). The hero never repeats the tagline's *beginning*. The Phase 5 lockup test is passed (the tagline holds beside the primary lockup on the kit cover); the Phase 9 usage search is the remaining check.

**The two phrases, decided (ledger AH):** *executive management* is the headline phrase — the tagline and any headline that names the role — because a cold reader understands it before knowing Bidaya's terms. *The executive layer* is the position phrase in every sentence of copy, the story and the tool. Considered and not carried for the headline: *executive team* (overclaims headcount); *executive support* (reads as executive-assistant services in the UAE market; vendor register); *executive decisions* (contradicts Rule 4 — you decide, Bidaya builds).

## 04 — Messaging hierarchy

**Level 1 — Brand headline** (hero; first impression; says what it does immediately per §9)

*Setup, operations, finance and tech — run inside your business by one firm.*

Alternate if the four-function line tests long in the hero: *Executive management for businesses that don't have it yet.* The hero never repeats the tagline's *beginning*.

*Retired from this level (ledger AD): "The executive layer a business cannot yet afford to hire." The economic logic stays as body content and on intent pages with a named benchmark; it never leads.*

**Level 2 — Supporting statement**

*Most businesses run for years before they can justify three senior hires. Until then, Bidaya runs setup, operations, finance and tech inside the business the way an executive would, monthly or for one defined piece of work, a few steps ahead of the owner.*

**The thesis line** (the name block; sits with or beneath Level 2 on the homepage)

*بداية means beginning. A business is decided at its start.*

**Level 3 — Key messages**

**1. One firm runs the whole operating side.**
Setup, operations, finance and tech, inside one engagement, by one team. Licensed specialists — tax consultants, accountants — are included in the fee, and everything is built on the tools the business already uses.
*Proof:* the four functions in a single engagement; specialists in the fee; "your tools, not new ones."

**2. Judgment before the sale.**
*Where you stand* returns a read on your business — what's covered, what needs building, what's yours to do, and what is due with the cost of missing it — in full, with no email required.
*Proof:* the ungated read; the obligations section with dated items and AED penalties; the honest closing line, including "you don't need an executive layer yet."

**3. Terms a firm that couldn't deliver would never publish.**
The first month is paid at full rate and carries no notice. Every engagement can end on thirty days' notice. Discovery beyond the first call is paid. When a business doesn't need a monthly engagement, Bidaya says so in writing.
*Proof:* the four mechanisms, each stated on How we work as policy.

**4. Handed back in a form that runs without us.**
Everything Bidaya builds is documented and owned by the client. The method is written down; what can run on its own is set up to. The do-it-yourself outcome is a real outcome, not a consolation.
*Proof:* the handover principle in every proposal; the method files delivered with the read.

**Level 4 — Proof bank** *(true today; placement rules in brackets)*

1. Setup, operations, finance and tech in one engagement; licensed tax consultants and accountants included in the fee.
2. *Where you stand*: the read in full, no email; what is due, with the AED cost of missing each item.
3. First month at full rate, no notice — finish it and walk away owing nothing.
4. Thirty-day exit on every engagement shape, including mid-term.
5. Unpaid discovery ends at the 30-minute call.
6. The written outcome: "you don't need us on retainer — here is what to do."
7. Monthly engagements from AED 12,000. *[How we work, intent pages, FAQ only — never the homepage hero, About or Programs.]*
8. Built on the tools the business already uses; handed back documented, in a form that runs with or without Bidaya.
9. The cost of a full-time executive, with a named source. *[Intent pages only. Draft 2 §3 gives AED 60–90K/month for a COO, finance lead, ops manager and IT function combined; Fractional \| Dubai cites Cooper Fitch 2025 at AED 61–92K/month base for a full-time CFO alone. Verify a source directly before publishing any figure; never copy a competitor's citation.]*
10. Anonymised engagements. *[Wording pending founder approval (ledger X); past tense only (F). The month-one engagement and the marine consultancy are usable once worded; ESK only after delivery.]*

## 05 — Audience-specific messaging

**Rania — founder, funded, monthly engagement**
- *Cares most about:* staying on the product; knowing the real unit economics; not acquiring a fourth job managing a junior finance hire.
- *Lead with:* message 1 — one firm runs the operating side — then message 3. She decides on mechanisms.
- *Proof that lands:* the trial month with no notice; reporting delivered on the tools she already uses; *Where you stand* before she's spoken to anyone.
- *Avoid:* venture idiom (runway, burn, raise); any menu or package; anything that reads as one person in a "we."

**Khalid — owner, established, one-time first**
- *Cares most about:* why margins moved; what he's exposed to; being treated as an established business, not a startup.
- *Lead with:* message 2 — what is due and what it costs — then the study: a fixed-scope piece of work that starts with discovery and hands back a structure that runs without Bidaya.
- *Proof that lands:* the obligations section; discovery as phase one of a fixed fee paid at milestones; "handed back in a form that runs with or without us."
- *Avoid:* "startup" and "founder" as address; "your company is a mess"; any sense the one-time path is the lesser one.

**Noor — head of programs, cultural institution — a different core message**
- *Core message for Programs:* *Business support inside the programs you already run, co-delivered with Bidaya — so your practitioners leave with a company, not only a portfolio.*
- *Cares most about:* outcomes she can report to a board; a partner in her register; her practitioners' viability.
- *Lead with:* partnership, not procurement — the program as a structure built together, co-funded with sponsors and partners, beneficiaries the practitioners.
- *Proof that lands:* the founding intent (the story's origin paragraph); Bidaya's method translated to practitioner scale; the institutions it works with, once they exist.
- *Avoid:* the executive-layer pitch; any price; the word "services"; startup coding. She never sees a floor.

## 07 — Things not to say

The full list is the rulebook's §08 and its checklist. The messaging-specific additions:

- **Don't lead with savings.** The salary comparison is the category's opening line; Bidaya's is the role. Savings appear on intent pages, with a named source, as a supporting fact.
- **Don't let "a few steps ahead" become "one step ahead of the competition."** It describes Bidaya's position relative to the *owner*, never to rivals.
- **Don't say "the only," "the first," or "no one else."** The map shows the quadrant is empty; the copy describes the structure — seat and hands — and lets the reader conclude.
- **Don't promise month one.** Past tense, approved wording, or nothing (F).
- **Don't put the floor where it doesn't belong.** Never the hero, About, story or Programs (Z, AA).
- **Don't describe Programs as anything Bidaya sells** (O).
- **Don't use a competitor's benchmark as if it were Bidaya's.** Verify, cite, or omit.
- **Don't stack the pillars into a triad.** Four messages, presented as four things, never as "not just X — but Y — and Z."
