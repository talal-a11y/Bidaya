**What this version is and isn't.** It is the mechanical half of the rulebook — rules with tests, banned lists, rewrite pairs, a checklist — built from what the record and the two written samples support. ## 01 — Voice overview

Bidaya writes the way an executive briefs an owner who is busy: it states the thing, gives the number, says what happens next and what stays with the owner, and stops. It has already thought about the next step, and it says so without ceremony. It never explains what the owner already knows and never implies they lack ability — it assumes competence and shortage of hours. It sounds serious because its claims are specific and its terms are published, not because it uses serious words. It is easy to reach and easy to read, and it is never the friend who helps out and never the vendor who sells.

**Voice essence:** *Bidaya sounds like the senior person in the room who has done the work before, writing to someone who is doing too much of it alone.*

## 02 — Tone dimensions

From the founder's sliders and the record. Each carries a working test.

**Casual ←——→ Formal — 6 of 8.** Formal-leaning, not stiff. Contractions allowed; slang, emoji and exclamation never. *Test:* would this read correctly in a proposal a board member picks up?
> *We'll set the reporting up in the tools you already use.*

**Playful ←——→ Serious — 6 of 8.** Serious, not generic. Dry understatement is the only permitted humour; jokes are not. *Test:* is the seriousness carried by specifics, or by adjectives? If adjectives, rewrite.
> *Build first and find out later is the prevailing advice, and it works for software.*

**Accessible ←——→ Authoritative — authoritative by evidence, accessible by construction.** Authority comes from numbers, mechanisms and named steps; accessibility from short sentences and no jargon. Never authoritative by tone alone. *Test:* strike every adjective about Bidaya — does the sentence still carry authority?
> *Unpaid discovery ends at the 30-minute call.*

**Calm ←——→ Energetic — calm.** Bold in position (8 of 8), calm in delivery. Energy comes from decisiveness, never from pace, hype or urgency. *Test:* is there a word in this sentence whose only job is excitement?
> *We would rather lose the client who wants to move without looking.*

**Distant ←——→ Warm — warm through attention, not affection.** Warmth is shown by anticipating what the owner needs and by giving decisions back to them; never by "we care," "we're here for you" or endearments. *Test:* does the warmth come from a thing Bidaya does, or a thing Bidaya says about itself?
> *The next filing, the next hire, the next requirement, surfaced before anyone has to ask.*

## 03 — The rules

Fifteen. Each is a behaviour with a test; none is an adjective.

**Rule 1 — Name the thing.** Zoho Books, not "your accounting platform." Week six, not "in the early stages." Corporate tax registration, not "compliance requirements." *Test:* could a competitor paste this sentence onto its own site unchanged? If yes, it isn't specific enough. *(Source: what survived the founder's edits of the ESK proposal.)*

**Rule 2 — State the number.** Where a number exists, use it; where it doesn't, don't fabricate a range to sound precise. The salary comparison cites a named benchmark or it doesn't run. *Test:* is there a figure in the sentence, and can Bidaya defend it?

**Rule 3 — The owner is stretched, never incapable.** The frame is always load, hours and a business growing past one person — never knowledge or ability. Never "you don't know how." Never "your company is a mess" (an owner may say it; Bidaya doesn't). *Test:* would this sentence embarrass an owner who is already doing everything? *(Ledger AA.)*

**Rule 4 — Give the decision back.** Every description of Bidaya's work ends with what stays with the owner. "You decide, Bidaya builds." "It supports decisions; it does not make them." *Test:* does the paragraph say what Bidaya does *and* what the owner keeps?

**Rule 5 — Decision first, reason second.** State the ruling, then the "because." *"We don't publish project prices. Scope decides them."* Not the reverse. *(Source: the founder's own construction throughout the questionnaire and rulings — "Monthly from 12K is fine — because…")* *Test:* does the sentence open on the conclusion?

**Rule 6 — Say no plainly.** Bidaya states what it doesn't do without softening: *We don't do marketing.* *We turn down work that isn't ready.* No "unfortunately," no "at this time." *Test:* is there a hedge word before the refusal?

**Rule 7 — Mechanism, not adjective.** Never claim a quality Bidaya could instead demonstrate. Not "we're honest" — the written "you may not need us" outcome. Not "we're committed" — the trial month with no notice. *Test:* delete the adjective; is there a mechanism left in the sentence? If not, add one or cut the sentence.

**Rule 8 — Past tense conditionally, future tense never.** Bidaya may describe what a past first month delivered (with approved wording); it never promises what a future one will. *Test:* does any sentence promise an outcome in a specific timeframe? *(Ledger F.)*

**Rule 9 — One "X, not Y" per page.** The construction is good once and a tell when stacked: the ESK proposal ran four in a page. *Test:* count them.

**Rule 10 — Plural always.** "We," never "I." The founder appears once, named, on the About page, and nowhere else in the voice. *Test:* search for "I " and "my ".

**Rule 11 — Headlines say what the page does.** No rhetorical questions the copy then answers; no cleverness that needs the body to decode. Sentence case. *Test:* would a reader know what the section contains from the headline alone? *(§8; §9 "says what it does immediately.")*

**Rule 12 — Address the owner, not the founder.** "Founders and owners" as the default address; "the owner" in the third person; "you" when speaking directly. No venture idiom — no runway, burn, raise, scale-up — outside intent pages where a query needs it. *Test:* would Khalid read this as written for him? *(Ledger H.)*

**Rule 13 — Category words live on intent pages.** "Fractional," "outsourced CFO," "part-time CFO" appear on intent pages, FAQ blocks and metadata, where search and AI citation need them, and nowhere else. The homepage and the story use "the executive layer"; only the tagline and role-naming headlines use "executive management." *Test:* is this an intent page? If not, the word doesn't belong. *(Ledger L, P4.)*

**Rule 14 — Programs is a partnership, never a service.** On the Programs path: partners, practitioners, institutions, co-delivered. Never "our services for institutions," never a price, never the executive-layer pitch. *Test:* could Noor read this as a vendor pitching her? *(Ledger O.)*

**Rule 15 — Stage, not size.** The client's business is described by where it is — before it has an executive layer, from the beginning, growing past one person — never by its means or its size. Never "cannot yet afford," "can't afford," "small," "a fraction of the cost" in a title, headline or key message; the economic logic lives in body copy and on intent pages with a named benchmark. *Test:* does the sentence describe the business by what it can't pay for? If yes, rewrite by stage. *(Ledger AD; founder: "cannot yet afford makes businesses feel small and positions us as the cheap alternative.")*

## 04 — Vocabulary

**Words we use**
- **the executive layer** — the position; always this phrase for what Bidaya is, in every sentence of copy. **Executive management** is the headline phrase only — the tagline and any headline that names the role — the plain-English entry a cold reader understands before the copy builds the layer (ledger AH). Never "executive support" (reads as assistant services), never "executive decisions" (contradicts Rule 4)
- **run, take, hold, prepare, build, hand back** — verbs for the work; not "assist," "help with," "provide"
- **the operating side** — setup, operations, finance, tech as one thing
- **setup, operations, finance and tech** — the four functions, in this order, every time (ledger AB, amended by AE). "Tech" everywhere: sentences, headlines and the descriptor lockup; never "technology"
- **the owner / founders and owners** — the reader
- **a few steps ahead** — the standard; use sparingly so it stays a phrase and not a slogan
- **done right / properly** — the two permitted words for done-right; the founder prefers "done right" (ledger AF). Guard: "done right" never sits beside "beginning" or "start" in a headline or tagline — that rebuilds §8's banned line. Never "best-in-class"
- **the beginning / بداية** — the thesis; the Arabic only beside the wordmark and in the name block
- **on the tools you already use** — the delivery principle
- **runs with or without us** — the handover principle
- **in writing** — how commitments are made
- **the study** — what happens before a recommendation; never "audit," never "assessment"
- **partners, practitioners, institutions, programs** — the Programs vocabulary
- **support** — the founder's word; permitted for Programs and the creative sector ("business support"); for the executive layer, prefer the verbs above

**Words we avoid — the full list is §08. Working shortlist:**
under one roof · one-stop shop · all your business needs · at a fraction of the cost · cannot yet afford / can't afford (in any title or key message) · small (as a description of the client's business) · without the overhead · senior leadership · strategic partner · trusted partner · tailored · bespoke · seamless · end-to-end · holistic · hustle · scale · elevate · empower · streamline · robust · leverage · navigate · unlock · transform · journey · passionate · world-class · solutions · affordable · hub · platform · assessment · scorecard · readiness

**Brand-specific language**
- The firm is **Bidaya**, with **بداية** in the lockup. Never "Bidaya Consulting," never "Bidaya LLC" in copy (legal line only).
- The four-function descriptor under the mark: *Setup · Operations · Finance · Tech*.
- Clients are **clients** (or "the owner," "the business"); never customers, users, accounts.
- Engagements are **monthly** or **fixed-scope**; never retainers, packages, plans or tiers on the site.
- The tool is ***Where you stand***; what it returns is *the read*, and its obligations section is *What is due* (decision 4, ledger AC). The publishing surface is **Reports and notes** — the two tracks named as the umbrella (assumed, ledger AU; Bidaya + plain descriptor; never "Blog," never "Insights").

## 05 — Writing style rules

**Sentence length:** short by default; one long sentence per paragraph is allowed when it carries a list of specifics. Never three short fragments in a row for rhythm — that is the AI tell the founder identified.

**Punctuation:** full stops and commas do most of the work. Colons for what-follows. Em dashes allowed, at most one per paragraph, never two in one sentence, never to build a three-part rhythm (§8). Semicolons rarely. Ellipses never. Exclamation marks never.

**Capitalisation:** sentence case everywhere — headlines, headers, buttons, table headers. No title case; no letter-spaced small-caps labels in copy (a template tell). Proper nouns only.

**Numbers:** numerals for money, time and quantities: AED 12,000 (never "12K" in copy), 30 days, week six, three senior salaries (spelled when it's a figure of speech rather than data). Currency always as "AED 12,000"; never "12,000 AED," never "Dhs."

**Contractions:** yes — we'll, doesn't, isn't — as the story uses them. Not in legal or terms lines.

**Voice:** active. Passive only when the actor is genuinely irrelevant ("historical transactions are posted").

**Emphasis:** italics for quoted owner sentences and Arabic transliteration; bold for at most one term per section; never bold-phrase-per-sentence.

**Humour:** dry understatement only, never a joke, never a pun, never a wink at the reader.

**Arabic:** بداية beside the wordmark and in the name block. No other Arabic words in English copy. Arabic pages, when they exist, are written or reviewed by a native professional, never machine-translated (§9).

## 06 — Channel adaptations

Voice constant, tone flexes.

| Channel | Adjustment | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Homepage | The hero says what Bidaya does; the tagline says what it is and when; name-meaning present; no category words | *Setup, operations, finance and tech — run inside your business by one firm.* / *Executive management, from the beginning.* |
| Intent pages | Category vocabulary permitted; FAQ blocks answer literal questions; the floor stated as policy | *Fractional CFO in Dubai: what it covers, what it costs, and when you don't need one.* |
| How we work | Mechanisms as proof; numbers; terms stated plainly | *The first month is paid at full rate and carries no notice.* |
| About / story | Who and why; no prices, no mechanics | *A business is decided at its beginning.* |
| Programs | Institutional register; partners and practitioners; no floor, no pitch | *Co-delivered with the institutions that host them.* |
| The tool | Second person; judgment, not scores; the do-it-yourself lines are the warmest copy on the site | *This one you can do yourselves. Here's the method.* |
| Proposals | Rule 1 at full intensity; the three-column judgment; tiers by scope; the founder's personal note is his | *What you identified. What Bidaya builds. What stays with you.* |
| Email | Shorter than the proposal, same voice; decision first; one ask per email | *Two things before Thursday. The Zoho access, and the licence copy.* |
| Reports and Notes | Reports: numbered, dated, a named source for every figure. Notes: what changed, what it means, what to do by when | *Corporate tax registration deadlines moved on 1 March. Here is what that costs if missed.* |
| Support / replies | Quick, plain, no scripted warmth | *Received. You'll have it Tuesday.* |

## 07 — Before and after

Every "before" is a real line from the founder's questionnaire or the ESK proposal.

**Before:** *Bidaya is a management and IT consultancy that works on supporting people and businesses across the UAE… all under one roof.*
**After:** *Bidaya is the executive layer for businesses that don't have one yet — one firm running setup, operations, finance and tech.*
*Why it works:* the category sentence is replaced by the position; "one roof" (the mill's phrase) becomes "one firm running" (the role).

**Before:** *I'm one of the only firms who manage all the services I'm offering under one roof.*
**After:** *Every firm in this market is either the seat without the hands or the hands without the seat. Bidaya is both.*
*Why it works:* the exclusivity claim (Rule 7, ledger J) becomes a structural description a competitor can't rebut.

**Before:** *I'm young fresh blood, I'm professional.*
**After:** *We publish our terms, because a firm that couldn't deliver wouldn't.*
*Why it works:* self-description swapped for a mechanism (Rule 7); "young" is a fact about the firm, not an argument for it (ledger N).

**Before:** *"I have a business but don't know how to manage it."*
**After:** *"It's getting too big to handle on my own."*
*Why it works:* Rule 3 — the same buyer, described by load instead of by lack.

**Before:** *Your Business. Fully Supported.*
**After:** *A few steps ahead.*
*Why it works:* vendor register (something done to you) replaced by an executive's standard (where Bidaya stands relative to you). Provisional tagline, Phase 4 messaging.

**Before:** *One team… structured to fit SMEs without the overhead of separate advisors.*
**After:** *One team across setup, operations, finance and tech. Licensed specialists are included in the fee.*
*Why it works:* ledger M's phrase removed; the mechanism (specialists in the fee) does the work the cliché was doing.

**Before:** *Honest Over Promotional* (as a section header)
**After:** *Timelines are stated as they are. Risks are named before they happen. The fee does not change.*
*Why it works:* honesty announced becomes honesty shown (Rule 7); the third sentence — the founder's own — is the strongest line in the proposal.

**Before:** *aspiring founders aged 25–40 who have a hustle mindset… want support in growing their business fast*
**After:** *Founders and owners carrying a business alone, who want the operating side off their desk and done properly.*
*Why it works:* Rule 12 (address), ledger H (idiom), and the audience reconciliation — speed of execution, not commitment.

## 08 — Things we never say

**The category sentence.** "Management and IT consultancy for SMEs, startups and creators" — anywhere, including metadata. The category noun appears only in the About page's machine-readable block.

**Exclusivity mechanisms** *(ledger J):* under one license · in-house · the only / one of the only · the first.

**The setup-mill register** *(ledger M):* one roof · one-stop shop · all your business needs · hassle-free · stress-free · in three easy steps · we handle the paperwork so you can focus.

**The fractional cliché set** *(ledger M):* at a fraction of the cost · without the full-time cost / commitment / overhead · senior financial / operational leadership · more than bookkeeping · scale up or down as your needs change · from brief to deployment in weeks · not just an adviser — an operator who embeds · strategic partner · trusted partner · your growth partner.

**AI-era tells** *(§8):* leverage (verb) · delve · navigate the complexities · in today's fast-paced landscape · transform / transformative · revolutionise · game-changing · elevate · robust · streamline · supercharge · unlock · empower · holistic · seamless · end-to-end · tailored / bespoke / customised solutions · data-driven insights · we don't just X, we Y · the em-dash triad · the rhetorical-question opener · the aphoristic pull-quote closing every section.

**Self-description** *(§7, N):* young · fresh · new school (as argument) · passionate · people person · reads rooms well · gets things done fast · we care · we're here for you · we deliver excellence · world-class · best-in-class.

**The incapable frame** *(AA):* you don't know how · we do it better · your company is a mess · let the experts handle it · leave it to us.

**Forbidden claims:** affordable · cheap · a promised first-month outcome (F) · any hub / platform / space language (Q) · any price outside How we work, intent pages and FAQ (Z, AA) · any price or "services" on the Programs path (O) · a client's name (§5) · the founder's credentials (§5) · the emirate or the office (AA).

**Forbidden phrasings of the thesis** *(§8, by the letter):* begin right · start right · the right beginning · build it right · from vision to reality. The thesis is carried as *from the beginning* — "Executive management, from the beginning." "Done right" and "properly" are both permitted in copy (AF); neither sits beside "beginning" or "start" in a headline.

**Format tells:** stat triads · title-case headers · letter-spaced small-caps labels · icon-heading-paragraph grids · numbered process ladders as page structure · emoji.

## The pre-ship checklist

Run on every piece — page, proposal, email, note — before it leaves. Mechanical: each item is a search or a count.

1. Search the §08 lists. Any hit is replaced, never caveated.
2. Count "X, not Y" constructions. Maximum one per page.
3. Count em dashes per sentence. Maximum one. Any three-part rhythm is rewritten.
4. Search "I " and "my ". Plural only, except the founder's personal note in proposals.
5. Find every adjective about Bidaya. Delete it or put a mechanism beside it.
6. Find every sentence about the owner. Does any imply lack of knowledge or ability? Rewrite by load. Does any describe the business by size or by what it can't pay for? Rewrite by stage.
7. Find every future-tense promise of a delivered outcome. Cut it.
8. Search "fractional," "outsourced," "part-time." Present only on intent pages, FAQ, metadata.
9. Search "AED." Present only on How we work, intent pages, FAQ. Never on About, story, or Programs.
10. Programs copy: search "service," "price," "from AED." Zero hits.
11. Search "hub," "platform," "space," "license," "in-house," "only," "technology." Zero hits. Search "executive management" — hits only in the tagline and role-naming headlines.
12. Headlines: does each say what follows? Any question mark in a headline is rewritten.
13. Read the piece aloud. Would the founder say this sentence to a client across a table? If not, rewrite until he would.

## 09 — What the voice note adds (deferred to round two — ledger W, AS)

This version can be applied by a careful writer and will produce copy that is correct and unlike the category. What it cannot produce is copy that is unmistakably Bidaya's, because the founder's own patterns live in speech. When a ten-minute voice note exists (explaining the ESK proposal to a friend: what it is, why the tiers, what he'd tell him not to buy) and is transcribed, the `brand-voice:guideline-generation` plugin runs on it and this rulebook gains:

- **Cadence:** his sentence length and rhythm when explaining, not writing.
- **Characteristic constructions:** the phrases he reaches for unprompted; the ones that survive from the questionnaire — "decision first, because second" — confirmed or extended.
- **How he handles objections:** what he says when a client pushes on price, on the trial month, on "why not just hire someone."
- **Warmth markers:** how attention and care actually sound in his mouth, so Rule 5 of the tone dimensions has real examples.
- **The keep/cut diff on the proposal:** five lines he'd keep, five he'd cut — the fastest extraction there is.

Until then, the questionnaire text stands as the founder-authored sample, and its two confirmed patterns are in the rules: decision first, reason second (Rule 5); say no plainly (Rule 6).
