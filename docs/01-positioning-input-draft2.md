## 1. What Bidaya Is

A management and IT consultancy for SMEs, tech startups, and creators in the UAE. Currently founder-led with a curated bench of freelancers and licensed partner firms; targeting a small hired team within twelve months.

**The name:** بداية — Arabic for *beginning*. This is not decoration. It is the organising idea of the business and should structure the site rather than appear as an etymology footnote.

**The core commercial engine: fractional leadership.** COO, CFO, CEO, CTO engagements where Bidaya sits inside the client business as its executive layer — operating model, financial leadership, technology direction, performance management, board-level reporting. Time-based, highest trust and highest margin.

**Retainers are the strongest shape of the offer — not the only one.** One-time engagements are a permanent, deliberate part of the model, not a waiting room for retainers. One-time work is faster to sell, faster to deliver, and resourced through freelance and temporary support rather than full-time hires — it therefore does not consume fractional capacity, which is what makes it a genuine second revenue line rather than a distraction from the first.

**The four supporting offers, framed as scope-of-role rather than a services menu:**

| Offer | What it is | Anchors a retainer? | Sold standalone? |
|---|---|---|---|
| Setup | Entity structure advisory and formation coordination | Entry point | Yes — one-time |
| Advisory | Strategy, business planning, operating model design | Paid discovery that can graduate to fractional | Yes — one-time |
| Finance & Accounting | Management reporting, budgeting, cash flow, systems design, tax/audit coordination via licensed partners | Retainer anchor | Yes — defined engagements |
| Operations & Recruitment | Process design, SOPs, org build-out, hiring roadmaps, placement | Retainer anchor | Yes — defined engagements |

An IT layer runs through all of it — custom platforms, databases, automation, AI tooling — built under Bidaya's own license rather than subcontracted. **One-time technical project work — web design, IT, software — is in scope,** priced from AED 5,000 (doubled from a previous 2,500).

**Deliberately not doing:** marketing, design and PR as sold services; **one-off brand projects** (brand work survives only inside ongoing retainers — note the distinction from web/IT/software project work, which is in scope); standalone formation/PRO work competing with formation mills; anything touching client funds; unpaid discovery beyond the initial 30-minute call.

**Critical framing note:** The sentence *"management and IT consultancy for SMEs, startups and creators offering setup, advisory, finance and operations"* is accurate and useless. It is indistinguishable from several hundred UAE firms and is precisely what an AI writes when given nothing. The site leads with **fractional leadership**. The four offers appear as what that executive does inside your business — never as a menu.

## 2. The Buyer

**Primary (the site is optimised for this person):** Founder or owner of a funded startup or existing SME, with capital, who needs an executive layer they cannot yet justify hiring full-time. **Monthly engagements range AED 15–30K depending on scope, with a published floor of AED 12K/month.** Mada-scale engagements (30K) sit near the top of the range — the top of the range, not the ceiling of what Bidaya will charge as the team grows. The floor exists because sub-12K monthly contracts (the old 4–8K tier) are no longer pursued.

**Secondary:** The **same buyer profile** — founders and owners of funded startups or existing SMEs — choosing a **one-time engagement** rather than a monthly one. Deal size ~AED 10K. This is not a lower-value audience; it is the same person choosing a different commitment shape. Structurally, the site does not need to address a second kind of person — it needs to offer a second shape of engagement to the same person.

**Tertiary (institutional and creative, different sales motion entirely):** Cultural institutions, educational institutions, and creative-industry founders and studios — Dubai Culture, Alserkal, Art Jameel, Sharjah Art Foundation and similar. Reached through Programs; converting from meetings and decks, not from the homepage. The Programs page exists as their landing destination and as ecosystem proof for the primary buyer.

**What happens if they don't hire Bidaya** — six failure states, in the buyer's own terms:

- Doing HR, finance, documentation, systems and people management themselves, at the expense of the actual business
- Hiring full-time too early — burning cash on junior-level management that does the job worse
- Missing structural steps that surface later as delay or rework cost
- Not reaching the right network of investors or partners
- **Running unprofitably without knowing why** — no management reporting, no visibility into unit economics
- **Fines and penalties from missed regulatory requirements** — tax, compliance, licensing, and equivalents in delivery work

The last two are the only failure states with a hard number attached. Under §3's "state the outcome, imply the mechanism" rule, that makes them the most usable of the six. They should not be softened into general risk language when they reach copy.

## 3. Positioning

**The core argument:** Bidaya is the executive layer a business cannot yet afford to hire.

**The supporting math (state the outcome, imply the mechanism):** A COO, a finance lead, an ops manager and an IT function for a funded startup in Dubai is roughly AED 60–90k/month in salary alone, before visas, EOS, benefits and recruitment cost. Bidaya delivers across all four functions at a fraction of that, with a 30-day cancellation clause — variable cost where a hire is a fixed one. Note: the revised 15–30K range *widens* the gap against the salary equivalent. The argument gets stronger, not weaker.

**Scope of role, not contract shape:** Fractional leadership describes what Bidaya does inside a business; it does not mean every engagement is a monthly retainer. The offer is defined by the role Bidaya plays, and that role can be engaged monthly or for a defined piece of work.

**On the AI leverage:** outcome loud, mechanism quiet but present. The argument is not "we use AI." It is that AI tooling in the hands of an experienced operator replaces headcount, and in the hands of an inexperienced one produces nothing. **Never use the phrase "AI-powered consultancy."** See Blacklist.

**On the cancellation clause:** keep the clause, change the argument. It is confidence in the work, not permission to leave. Do not frame it as "like an employee minus benefits."

**Price position:** value-priced, not cheap. ⚠️ Never use the word "affordable" on the site regardless of how true it is.

## 4. Point of View — The Enemy

*(Unchanged from Draft 1 by explicit instruction.)*

**The primary enemy: build-then-find-out.**

The ship-fast, launch-and-learn orthodoxy applied to businesses that are not software. For a new venture, research-backed planning before commitment. For an existing business, a study of how it actually runs — finances, teams, sales, process — before recommending a single improvement. Both take time, and the market is impatient about time.

This position has a real cost, which is what qualifies it as a position: it loses the founder who wants to move now, and it means turning down work that isn't ready.

**The secondary enemy: hiring a consultancy too late.**

The prevailing belief is that consultants are a later-stage luxury. The counter-argument: the earliest stage is when the work is worth the most, because the cost of the missed step compounds. Hiring early does not burn capital, it protects it.

This answers three questions Bidaya's buyers actually ask, in their own words:
- *"I have an idea but no clue where to begin"*
- *"I know what business I want to run, I just don't know how"*
- *"I want to set up, but I don't know if this is the right market for it"*

**Deprioritised:** "people who refuse to adopt AI" — too generic to function as an enemy, and describes a share of the buyer set.

**Off-strategy for the main site:** "it doesn't take much capital to start" — true, useful for creatives, and in direct tension with premium positioning. Route it to Programs.

## 5. Proof — Entity Credibility, No Names, No CV

**Constraints:** No client names, no logos, no testimonials, no attributed case studies, no "trusted by" strip, no "proven track record." **And no founder credentials:** Bidaya is an LLC intended to outgrow its founder and build its own credibility as an entity. Credentials are shared in person, in meetings, with clients — never published. A site led by one person's CV makes the firm a person, which is the opposite of what is being built.

**The proof hierarchy, ranked by strength:**

1. **The qualification tool.** A free tool on the site returning a genuinely useful read on a founder's business, while capturing qualification data for the enquiry flow. This is judgment demonstrated *before the sale*, attributable to the firm rather than a person — it substitutes for a CV better than a CV does, because it proves Bidaya can think about *their* business specifically rather than proving someone once had a good job. Simultaneously: top proof asset, qualification mechanism for §6, and a build item. ⚠️ What it is and what it returns is an open decision — see §11, including the warning about the predictable version.

2. **Published reports and analysis.** Credibility earned by publishing work rather than citing a background. UAE-market-specific, not general business commentary. **Not a launch dependency** — the site launches without them — but a build-in-parallel programme intended to be ready when publishing begins. Until reports exist, the tool and the mechanisms below carry the external-validation load. ⚠️ Cadence, format, subjects, and authorship are open — see §11.

3. **Published commercial mechanisms as proof-by-commitment.** The price floor, the paid-discovery policy, the written "you may not need us on retainer" outcome, the 30-day clause. A firm that could not deliver would not publish these. §6 is doing proof work that must be counted and presented as proof, not buried as terms.

4. **Anonymised engagements.** Month-one-from-zero (in ~30 days: licensing, 90-day plan, financial forecast, feasibility begun, hiring plan, founding-team job posts) and the marine consultancy (freezone licensing, CT registration and FTA certificate, financial assessment, systems migration). Retained **conditional on wording** — founder's note: "maybe if worded right." The phrasing is an open craft problem, not a settled asset.

5. **Programs and institutional collaborations** as ecosystem proof. Founder's strongest endorsement: "this is going to help significantly."

**The compounding risk, stated plainly and not softened:** with credentials withdrawn, no client nameable, no founder visible, and reports not yet published, the tool and the commercial mechanisms carry the entire credibility burden at launch. Draft 1's warning — that a plural "we" concealing a solo operator can read as inflated to exactly this buyer — was sound and is carried forward as an acknowledged risk, not a deleted one. The decision stands; the risk stands; the mitigation is the tool at launch and publishing soon after.

**On over-delivery:** The month-one intensity is a proof asset, not an operational error. Do not throttle delivery to protect engagement tenure. Solve it commercially: minimum terms, milestone-weighted fees, or explicit phase definitions. ⚠️ Contract structure decision pending.

## 6. Commercial Structure

**The funnel:**

1. Free 30-minute call — qualifier, no obligation
2. Paid diagnostic/advisory step — ⚠️ needs a name and a price
3. Proposal — retainer or one-time, whichever the diagnostic concludes

**Published on the site:** floors, not menus. "Project work from AED 5,000." "Monthly engagements from AED 12,000." Range communicated as scope-dependent (15–30K typical). ⚠️ The three numbers — project floor, diagnostic price, retainer floor — must be priced together as one ladder: the diagnostic has to sit comfortably between 5K and 12K or the ladder has a missing rung. One pricing session, not three.

**Why publish floors:** the scarcest resource is founder discovery time. A floor filters before the call.

**Publish the paid-discovery policy explicitly.** "Every engagement begins with a paid advisory step" qualifies hard and signals the time has value before anything is signed.

**The mechanism, not the claim:** the diagnostic's stated possible outcome includes *"you don't need us on retainer — here is what to do yourself."* In writing, in the deliverable description.

**Capacity is elastic, not fixed.** Capacity rises with hiring, and hiring is triggered by revenue, not client count: **hiring begins on the next retainer above AED 10K/month.** The hire is not assigned to that client — the hire absorbs support work so founder time goes to client work, which is what makes lower per-client deal sizes reduce concentration in practice: the slot count rises on a defined trigger. Current margins support this — the firm's running costs are a small fraction of revenue, so a single mid-range retainer funds a hire.

**One-time engagements are resourced through freelance and temporary support and do not consume fractional capacity.**

**The site is built to handle 50 enquiries** — with qualification (budget, stage, timeline, engagement shape) embedded in the enquiry flow so 50 enquiries produce ~6 calls.

## 9. Visual Direction

Derived from reactions to seven reference sites. These are constraints, not a mood board.

### Locked

| Decision | Rationale |
|---|---|
| **Rounded corners throughout** — cards, buttons, containers | Consistent positive reaction (Plaid); consistent negative reaction to sharp-edged/boxy layouts |
| **Dark primary colour, not pure black** | Dark reads premium; pure black rejected as oppressive. Deep saturated tone, not pastel |
| **Says what it does immediately** | BCG's delayed reveal works for BCG only. Bidaya has no name recognition to trade on |
| **Motion and interactivity present but restrained** | Plaid's mouse-responsive hue shifts admired but "maybe a little too much." Calibrate below Plaid |
| **Rigorous responsive behaviour at every viewport** | Contractbook's failure — template-pasted-on-background appearance — is the specific thing to avoid |
| **Semantic, well-structured HTML** | The site will be read by AI research tools, not only humans |

*(Repository note: "dark primary colour" was amended in Phase 5 to a light system — warm stone page, dark ink text, coloured bands — ledger AJ; and the "restrained" motion ceiling was reopened in Phase 8 — ledger AX. See CLAUDE.md.)*

### The escape clause

If several genuine attempts within these constraints fail to produce work that meets the bar, a §9 constraint may be challenged — **but only by asking first and receiving explicit founder approval.** The request must name the specific constraint, why it is blocking, and what breaking it buys. Never broken silently; never broken and explained afterwards.

**This clause is scoped to §9 only and does not extend to §8.** The §8 blacklist is not a design preference — it is the definition of the failure mode. §9 is a set of derived preferences, and preferences should bend when they stop serving.

### Excluded

- Orange as a primary colour
- BCG's secondary green
- Pure white dominant backgrounds
- Emoji in interface or copy
- Inter / Poppins / Montserrat / Open Sans (see Blacklist)

### Typography

Stated preferences point toward **type with character** — bold display faces with a readable body — and away from neutral geometric sans. ⚠️ Type pairing undecided. Require three alternatives with a defence of each.

### Wordmark ⚠️

Standalone mark + lockup, resolved as separate problems: the mark must survive alone at favicon size, in single colour, with a distinctive silhouette; the lockup carries BIDAYA and بداية. Test at least three bilingual lockups before discarding the approach — the length objection applied to the mark, not the lockup, and بداية is five compact letters. An Arabic name that means something, rendered bilingually, in a market where the competitive set is called things like "Gateway Business Setup," is the strongest identity-level differentiator available.

### Name meaning placement

On the homepage, not buried in About. *Beginning* is the thesis of the business; it should structure the page, not footnote it.

### Language

English at launch. **Build bilingual-ready** — RTL-capable layout, no hardcoded direction assumptions, Arabic type stack selected even if unused. Arabic copy written or reviewed by a native professional when the institutional audience becomes a priority.

## 10. Site Architecture — Preliminary

Single site. Creatives and institutions are reached through Programs. No hidden or unlisted pages as segmentation. A standalone campaign landing page is appropriate for the university competition when it launches.

Provisional structure:

- **Home** — fractional leadership as the lead argument; name-meaning on this page
- **What we do** — scope-of-role framing, not a services menu; both engagement shapes visible
- **How we work** — the diagnostic, the paid-advisory step, the cancellation clause, the no-overselling mechanism, the published floors (§5 proof asset 3 presented as proof, not terms)
- **⚠️ Publishing surface** — reports and analysis need a home. Added by amendment; the most significant structural change from Draft 1. Naming and placement open (not "Blog" — that undersells the proof function)
- **⚠️ The qualification tool** — needs a placement decision: its own page feeding Start, or a homepage position. It is simultaneously the top proof asset (§5), the qualification mechanism (§6), and a build item (Phase 8). A real decision, not an implementation detail
- **Programs** — universities, creative zones, cultural institutions, brand partnerships
- **⚠️ About — reconsidered.** With credentials gone and the founder not visible, the Draft 1 spec ("founder credentials, the firm's thesis") loses half its content. Either it becomes the firm's thesis alone, or it merges into Home/How we work
- **Start** — qualified enquiry flow (budget, stage, timeline, **engagement shape**). Must route retainer and one-time paths without making one look like a failed version of the other
