## 01 — What Bidaya Is

**The position.** Bidaya is the executive layer for businesses that don't have one yet. One firm operating at CEO, CFO and COO scope across setup, operations, finance and tech — the seat *and* the hands — engaged monthly or for a defined piece of work. Where a need falls outside its scope, it knows who does it and manages them. It works a few steps ahead of the owner, and it publishes the terms that prove it.

**The origin — kept separate, because it is a different audience.** Bidaya was started with the creative field in mind — freelancers, young aspiring entrepreneurs, and studios: the people least likely to be offered serious support and most likely to need it. The executive-layer work for funded companies is what pays for that, and it became the commercial engine. Both are true. The site leads with the position; the story carries the origin; neither pretends the two audiences are one.

**The name is the thesis.** بداية — beginning. A business is decided at its start; the structural step done now is the fine, the rework and the lost quarter avoided later. This belongs on the homepage, structuring it, not in About as etymology (Draft 2 §9).

**Not the category sentence.** "A management and IT consultancy for SMEs, startups and creators offering setup, advisory, finance and operations" is accurate and unusable anywhere, including metadata (Draft 2 §1; ledger I).

## 02 — Vision

*Founder-stated; horizon ruled 2–4 years.*

Within two to four years, Bidaya becomes the place where the UAE's founders, creatives and young businesses learn from one another and get their businesses run properly: a platform for SMEs to manage what they've built, for freelancers to find work, for creative practitioners to get business support — and in time a physical space where that happens.

**Guardrail, binding:** none of this appears on the site at launch. A firm that calls itself a hub or platform before one exists undercuts the executive-layer argument and the price floor. **Architecture ruling (§13):** when the platform exists it takes its own name, endorsed — *[Name] by Bidaya* — and is not pre-named.

Pillars: Beginning · Knowledge · Support · Platform

## 04 — Values

*Translated from the founder's stated values. "We help others" and "we support" are the job, not values; "deliver excellence" is a hollow adjective. Neither is carried. Provisional until Phase 4 voice work confirms the words; the substance is locked.*

**As our own.** The businesses Bidaya works inside are treated as its own — an executive's stance, not a vendor's. The value-level form of the positioning.

**Properly, from the beginning.** Research before commitment; a study of how a business runs before a recommendation; the structural step now rather than paid for later. It costs the client who won't wait, and the cost is the position.

**Ahead.** Anticipation as a standard — the next filing, the next hire, the next requirement, surfaced before the owner asks.

**Whole.** The business, not the function. The executive layer across all of it, and the network managed for the rest.

## 06 — Audience

Three audiences, one register (ledger H). "Founders and owners," never "founders" alone; no startup-exclusive idiom. The primary converts from the site, the secondary chooses a shape on it, the tertiary verifies through it.

**The reconciliation that governs the ICP.** The founder's "hustle mindset, wants to grow fast" and Draft 2's "loses the founder who wants to move now" are both true about different speeds. This buyer wants speed of **execution** — things off their plate, done properly. They are not buying speed of **commitment** — the study skipped. "Hustle" is internal shorthand and never copy. "Aspiring" routes to Programs.

**Primary profile:** founder or owner of a trading, capitalised UAE business — seed-funded or revenue-funded — carrying the whole company personally at the expense of the thing they're good at. Age roughly 25–45 (descriptive, never a rule). Sharjah, Dubai, Abu Dhabi. Engagement budget AED 12–30K/month, or a fixed-scope project quoted after the call (internally: small tech projects from AED 5,000; structuring and advisory projects typically AED 40–80K over 3–6 months — never published, ledger Z). Decides on mechanisms; reads the whole How-we-work page; books a call only when a number and a term are on it. Trigger: a failure state with a number on it — a fine, an unexplained losing quarter, an investor asking for reporting that doesn't exist.

**Rania, 32 — founder, seed-funded logistics-software company, Dubai.** Nine staff, eighteen months in. Product and customers by day; invoices, visas and an unwritten hiring plan by night. *"I know exactly what the product needs. I have no idea if the company is profitable."* Needs the executive layer that reports to her, not a vendor that bills her. Converts on the floor, the trial month with no notice, and the written "you may not need us." Hesitates at any menu, package, or a "we" that is one person. → Monthly engagement.

**Khalid, 41 — owner, contracting and fit-out business, Sharjah.** Twelve years, forty staff, run from his phone. Margins moved and nobody can say why; his accountant records, no one explains. *"The business is good. The company is a mess."* Resents being called a startup. Needs a study of how his company actually runs before anyone recommends anything. Converts on the paid diagnostic, priced and described, with the do-it-yourself outcome in writing. Hesitates at hoodie language or a startup-agency look. → One-time diagnostic first; a term engagement only if that's the honest conclusion. **He is why the site reads to owners, not only founders.**

**Noor, 36 — head of programs, cultural foundation, Dubai.** A residency of practitioners excellent at their craft and lost on invoicing, licensing, pricing; a board that wants viable businesses. *"We can teach them craft. We can't teach them to run a company."* Needs a partner to co-deliver inside her program, in her institution's register. Verifies through the site; converts in a meeting. Hesitates at any floor, package or startup coding on the Programs path. → Programs. She never sees a price.

**Their language — usable near-verbatim, and the antidote to the category's:** "I have an idea but no clue where to begin." "It's getting too big to handle on my own." "Too much on my plate." "Done properly." "A few steps ahead of me." "Stay on the business." "Not get fined for something I didn't know about."

**Not for:** the pre-capital idea-stage founder (→ Programs and the tool's do-it-yourself outcome); the founder who wants to skip the study; sub-12K monthly budgets for ongoing work; the formation-mill buyer; anyone wanting a marketing, design or PR agency; companies with a full C-suite; institutions expecting fractional services.

**Round-two gap:** every channel and community claim above is a hypothesis — there is no customer research. Ten client conversations would replace the hypotheses with facts.

## 09 — Competitive Position

*Evidence in the Phase 2 landscape summary; not restated.*

The market is two markets that never touch. Accounting-led firms (Aviaan, Finanshels, Kaizen, Al Burhan, BCL) bundle setup with bookkeeping and put a "CFO" label on top — hands, no seat. Executive-led operators (Fractional | Dubai, CAPG, The CFO Centre, Growwth) place genuine senior people and deliver nothing beneath the seat — seat, no hands. On the map — executive scope against delivery breadth — the high-scope, broad-delivery quadrant is empty. Bidaya sits there.

**The moat, honestly:** structural first (a competitor would have to change its business model, not its marketing), commitment second (only a firm that can deliver can publish these terms), judgment third (the tool). Not permanent — Fractional | Dubai adding delivery is the obvious threat (ledger R10). The durable moat is proof accumulated.

**Decision 10: closed, verified.** The claim is role-based and mechanism-silent: *one firm operating as the executive layer across setup, operations, finance and tech.* Never "under one license," "in-house," "the only," "one roof," "all your business needs."

## 10 — Positioning Statement

**Framework:** *For founders and owners of funded startups and trading SMEs in the UAE, Bidaya is the executive layer before they can hire one: one firm operating at CEO, CFO and COO scope across setup, operations, finance and tech. It runs the business rather than advising it, and publishes the terms that prove it.*

**Strategic (internal):** Bidaya occupies the one position in the UAE market nobody holds: executive-scope leadership with full delivery beneath it. Engaged monthly or for a defined piece of work; the role is the same in either shape. Where a need falls outside its scope, it knows who does it and manages them. It proves seniority by commitment and demonstration — published floors, a waived-notice trial month, a written do-it-yourself outcome, a tool that returns judgment — because the category has exhausted assertion. It is named for what it believes: a business is decided at its beginning.

**Human (public register):** Bidaya runs the parts of your business you can't hire for yet — setup, operations, finance, tech — the way an executive would, a few steps ahead of you. Engage us monthly, or for one piece of work. The terms are published, the first month has no strings, and if you don't need us, we'll say so in writing.

**One sentence:** *Bidaya is the executive layer for UAE businesses that don't have one yet — one firm running setup, operations, finance and tech from the beginning, on terms it publishes.*

**Tagline:** *Executive management, from the beginning.* **Hero headline:** *Setup, operations, finance and tech — run inside your business by one firm.* (Ledger AE. The Phase 5 lockup test is passed — the tagline holds beside the primary lockup on the kit cover; the Phase 9 usage search is outstanding.)

**Constraint check:** no "fractional" in the lead (L); no license / in-house / only / one roof (J, M); no "affordable" or "cannot afford" — stage, not size (AD); "founders and owners" (H); §8 clean. Red flags: none — the position can't apply to any competitor, the audience is specific, the differentiator is not a table stake, and it is substantiable at launch by mechanisms and the tool, fully once reports and anonymised engagements are in place.

## 12 — Programs: The Second Position

Bidaya's position in a second, nearly empty space: business and growth support for the cultural and creative sector, where Alserkal Advisory does cultural production and no one does the rest. Not fractional leadership through another door. Alserkal and the institutions are partners and target clients, not competitors.

**Definition (ledger O, confirmed):** a program is a partnership structure co-delivered with institutions and businesses, whose beneficiaries are creative businesses and independent practitioners.

**Product — as proposed and not amended:** a defined, time-bound structure inside a partner's existing program or as a joint launch — a business-foundations track inside a residency or cohort; a clinic series (setup, pricing, invoicing, licensing, tax basics); a competition or challenge format with a university; an initiative co-launched with a business partner. Bidaya's role: designer and delivery partner — the executive-layer expertise at practitioner scale. Beneficiaries receive Bidaya's work; they are not clients at Bidaya's floors. The partner gets a reportable outcome; Bidaya gets ecosystem proof, the founding mission served, relationships with institutions that are also target clients, and — never pushed — practitioners who may later become clients on their own terms.

**Rulings (founder, Phase 3):** funding goal **co-funded — Bidaya + sponsors + partners**; **mission and proof for now**, funded by the executive work, outside the 60K MRR goal; branding goal **co-branded or Bidaya-branded-and-hosted**, with **partner-branded, Bidaya credited** expected early.

**Never:** a discounted route to fractional leadership; a price-tabled offer to institutions; lead generation dressed as support.

## 13 — Brand Architecture

**Model: hybrid — branded house at the core, endorsed where a second brand must sit beside Bidaya's.** Entity credibility is the whole proof strategy; every asset deposits into one name. Programs are co-delivered, so each program is an endorsed co-brand by nature. The platform serves a different audience and price register, so it takes its own name, endorsed, when it exists.

```
BIDAYA — بداية                                the firm; the executive layer
├── the executive layer (monthly / fixed-scope) descriptor only — scope of role, never products
│     └── one-time technical projects         descriptor
├── Where you stand                           Bidaya + descriptor — the read; decision 4 closed
├── [the publishing surface]                  Bidaya + descriptor — name: Phase 4; not "Blog"
├── Programs                                  branded house at the umbrella
│     └── each program                        endorsed co-brand — [Program] · [Partner] × Bidaya; host first
└── [the platform] (2–4 yrs)                  endorsed — [Name] by Bidaya; own register; not pre-named
```

**Naming rules:** Bidaya + plain descriptor for the firm's own assets; descriptors describe, never sell; no tiers on the site or in product names (proposals tier by scope — ledger Z); nothing from §8 or ledger M in a name (no hub, launchpad, accelerator, readiness, scorecard); Arabic at the parent level only; geography never in a name; the platform not named until it exists.

**Decision tree for anything new:** same buyer → *Bidaya + descriptor*. Different audience with a partner present → *endorsed co-brand, host first*. Different audience, no partner, premium register would confuse it → *own name, by Bidaya*. Different audience, no partner, register helps → *Bidaya + descriptor*.

**Names the site needs before Phase 7:** *Where you stand* (decided); the publishing surface — two tracks, Reports and Notes (ledger V), umbrella name open; each program's title (as created).

**Visual:** one system, ruled in Phase 5 (identity system spec v1.0): the mark kept exactly; mirrored bilingual lockup as primary; the partner rule for Programs — Bidaya's mark in single colour at the partner's visual height, a hairline between, host first (assumed accepted, ledger AT). The platform's own accent is decided when the platform exists. Executive-management messaging transfers to the firm's assets; not to Programs (institutional register) or the platform (access register). The thesis — beginning — transfers everywhere.

**Governance:** the founder owns brand decisions; the decision tree runs before any launch; the architecture is reviewed at every new program and once, deliberately, when the platform is greenlit.

- Programs is not services through another door.
