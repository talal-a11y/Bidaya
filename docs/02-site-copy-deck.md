## 2. What we do

**Title:** What we do — Bidaya
**Description:** One firm operating at CEO, CFO and COO scope across setup, operations, finance and tech. Engaged monthly, or for one defined piece of work.

`[H1]` The executive layer, run inside your business

A funded company or an established business reaches a point where the operating side is too much for one person and too early for three senior hires. Bidaya fills that layer. We operate at CEO, CFO and COO scope across setup, operations, finance and tech, inside the business, reporting to the owner. The owner keeps every decision that counts. We prepare the ground so those decisions are made on numbers, earlier, with less on the owner's desk.

`[H2]` What the role covers

`[note: four rows, plain, no grid of icons:]`

**Setup.** Entity structure and the licensing decision. Formation coordinated with the specialists it needs. The foundations decided once, so they don't have to be redone at the first hire, the first investor or the first audit.

**Operations.** How the business runs when the owner isn't in the room: process written down, the people plan, the hiring roadmap and the hires themselves, the review rhythm that keeps the month on track.

**Finance.** Management reporting every month, budgeting and cash flow, the reporting an investor or a bank asks for, and the filings that carry a fine when they're missed. Licensed tax consultants and accountants are included in the fee.

**Tech.** The systems under all of it: the accounting stack, the records, the automation that removes a job rather than adds a tool. Built on what the business already uses. One-time technical projects, from a website to a custom internal system, are in scope on their own.

Where a need sits outside these four, we know who does it and we manage them. The client has one firm to call.

`[H2]` Two shapes of the same role

**Monthly.** The executive layer inside the business on an ongoing basis. Rolling, on thirty days' notice, or on a term matched to a deadline. This is the shape most engagements take once the foundations exist.

**One defined piece of work.** A fixed scope, a fixed fee, a beginning and an end. A study of how the business actually runs. A structure built and handed over. A technical build. Discovery is the first phase of every one, inside the fee, and the work is handed back in a form that runs without us.

Neither is the lesser version of the other. Which shape fits is what the first call decides, and *Where you stand* will usually have told you before the call.

We don't do marketing, design or PR as sold work. We don't do one-off brand projects. We don't do formation on its own, in competition with the formation mills. We don't touch client funds. Most consultancies publish nothing and explain everything on a call. We publish the terms, because the terms are the proof: a firm that couldn't deliver against them wouldn't put them in writing.

Every monthly engagement begins with a full month at the full monthly rate, and the thirty-day notice is waived for it. Finish month one and walk away owing nothing further. There is no setup fee and no weighted first month. The price of month one is the price of every month.

Rolling engagements have no term. Term engagements, for work that has to hit a date, run six or twelve months, reserve capacity and lock the rate, and can still end on thirty days' notice. Fixed-scope projects are paid at milestones, and the milestone is the exit. The cost is variable where a hire is fixed, and there is no asterisk.

`[H2]` Monthly engagements from AED 12,000

That is the published floor for the executive layer inside a business. What sits above it depends on scope: how many of the four functions, how much of each, how often. Project work is scoped and quoted after the call. Proposals set out the options by scope; the site does not.

`[H2]` Unpaid discovery ends at the thirty-minute call

*Where you stand* and the first call carry the discovery for most engagements, and most go straight to a proposal. Nothing beyond the first call is free, and nothing beyond it is vague.

`[H2]` When you don't need us, we say so in writing

The read, the study and every proposal can conclude that a business doesn't need a monthly engagement. When that's true, it's written down, with what to do instead. One defined piece of work is a full outcome, not a consolation. So is doing it yourselves, with our method.

`[H2]` Handed back in a form that runs without us

Everything we build is documented and owned by the client: the reporting pack, the registers, the process, the systems. What can run on its own is set up to. If we leave on thirty days' notice, the business keeps working.

`[H2]` Payment

Monthly engagements are paid before the month begins. Projects are paid at milestones, typically at signing, at a mid-point deliverable and at handover, and work begins on the first payment. Quarterly and annual options exist; ask.

## 7. About

**Title:** About — Bidaya

`[H1]` Why Bidaya exists

Two sentences reach us more than any others. *I have an idea and no idea where to begin.* And, from people further along: *It's getting too big to handle on my own.*

They sound like different problems. They aren't. In both cases the business is outgrowing what one person can carry, and the people saying them are not short of ability. They are short of hours, and of the layer of a company that established companies take for granted: someone whose job is the structure, the process, the numbers and the systems, so the owner's time goes to the work the business exists to do.

Most businesses in the UAE run for years without it, until they can justify three senior salaries. Until then the owner does it, at night, between everything else; or a junior hire does it badly; or it waits, and the cost arrives later: a fine for a filing nobody flagged, a quarter that lost money without anyone being able to say why, a hire that should have happened a year earlier. None of that is a failure of the owner. It is what happens when the operating side of a company has no one whose job it is.

Bidaya was started with the people least likely to be offered serious support and most likely to need it in mind: freelancers in the creative field, young aspiring entrepreneurs, and studios. What became clear quickly is that the same gap existed at funded companies too, where the cost of its absence was larger and the owner's time more expensive. So the firm was built to do one thing at both scales: be the executive layer for businesses that don't have one yet. For funded startups and established SMEs, that is our work. For the creative sector, it is our programs.

`[H2]` The name

بداية, Bidaya, is the Arabic word for *beginning*, and we chose it because a business is decided at its beginning: by whether the structure exists before the growth does, whether the numbers are visible before the decision is made, whether the study happens before the launch. The market is impatient about this. Build first and find out later is the prevailing advice, and it works for software. It does not work for a contracting company, a clinic or a studio, where the missed step is not a bug to patch but a cost that compounds. We would rather lose the client who wants to move without looking than build on a foundation we haven't seen.

`[H2]` What we do inside a business

We take the load an executive would take, so the owner's time goes back to what matters. We run the operating side: structure and setup, operations and hiring, finance and reporting, the tech underneath. Where a need sits outside our scope, we know who does it and we manage them. The owner keeps every decision that counts: pricing, clients, quality, money. We prepare the ground so those decisions are made on numbers, earlier, with less on the owner's desk. A few steps ahead is the standard we hold ourselves to: the next filing, the next hire, the next requirement, surfaced before anyone has to ask. None of this is because an owner can't. It's because an owner shouldn't have to, at the expense of the business.

We publish our terms, because a firm that couldn't deliver wouldn't. They are written for the client's confidence rather than ours: when a business doesn't need us monthly, we say so in writing and hand over what to do instead, and everything we build is documented, owned by the client, and left in a form that runs with or without us.

`[H2]` The firm

Bidaya was founded by Talal Abdel-Hamid and runs with a small team and a bench of licensed specialists. The firm is built to outgrow its founder: the credibility we're interested in is the firm's, earned inside the businesses it works with.

We're based in the UAE. If you're carrying a business alone and want the operating side off your desk, or you're building something for the creative sector and want a partner in it, the beginning is when to talk.

`[H1]` Fractional COO in the UAE: what it covers, what it costs, and when you don't need one

The owner of a growing business ends up running operations by default: the process, the people, the hiring, the week. A fractional COO takes that role for part of the time a full-time one would, inside the business. At Bidaya the role is one scope of the executive layer, run alongside setup, finance and tech by one firm, rather than a seat on its own.

`[H2]` What it covers

The way the business runs when the owner isn't in the room. Process written down for the work that repeats: delivery, handover, variations, quality. The people plan and the hiring roadmap: who is needed next, by when, and what they'll do. The hires themselves, from the role description to the offer. The review rhythm: a weekly format the team runs, and a monthly one the owner reads. And the registers that keep the company out of trouble: renewals, expiries, obligations.

`[H2]` What it costs

Monthly engagements from AED 12,000. What sits above the floor depends on scope: operations alone, or operations with finance and tech inside one engagement. Project work, such as writing the process and the registers for a business and handing them over, is scoped and quoted after the call.

`[H2]` When you don't need one

When the business has fewer than a handful of people and the owner can still hold the week in their head. Then the answer is process written down and a review format, both of which *Where you stand* hands you with the method. When the business is past that, the question is whether one defined piece of work covers it or the role needs to be there monthly. The read will tell you which; so will the call.

`[H2]` How it starts

`[H2]` Questions

**What is a fractional COO?** An operations executive engaged for part of the time a full-time one would give, inside the business, with the authority and the reporting of the role. At Bidaya the role is engaged monthly on thirty days' notice, and the first month carries no notice.

**Fractional COO or operations manager?** An operations manager runs the day. A COO decides how the day should run, builds the process and the team, and reports to the owner on whether it's working. Businesses usually need the second before they can afford the first full-time.

**Does Bidaya place a COO in my company?** No. Bidaya is the executive layer as a firm: one firm doing the work, with a bench of licensed specialists included where the work needs them. The client has one firm to call, and the work is handed back in a form that runs without us.

**How long does it take to see the difference?** We don't promise what a month will deliver. We publish the terms instead: a first month with no notice, and thirty days' exit on every engagement. The client decides whether it's working, monthly.

**Can I start with a project instead?** Yes. One defined piece of work is a full outcome, not a step toward a monthly engagement. Many businesses need the process built once and then run it themselves.

Management reporting every month: profit, cash, margins by line of work, in a pack the owner reads in ten minutes. Budgeting and the cash-flow view. Monthly engagements from AED 12,000, scope-dependent above that.

How the business actually runs: finances, teams, sales, process, systems. Every improvement we recommend follows that study, never precedes it. When the question is one *Where you stand* can answer in ten minutes. Try that first. When the decision is already made and what's needed is the setup itself, that's a project, and the study is its first phase rather than a separate piece.
